# Micro-research post template (~1,000 words)

Write one section at a time. Answer the questions in your own words; don't
try to sound polished. Paste each section for feedback, lock it, move on.

---

## 1. Why I looked at this (~100 words)
- What were you working on when this question came up?
- What decision were you trying to make?
- Why would someone building similar things care?

## 2. What I found (~75 words)
- If a reader stops here, what's the one thing they should know?
- What's the single most striking number?

## 3. What I expected (~100 words)
- What did you think would happen before you ran anything?
- Where did that expectation come from (a paper, experience, a hunch)?

## 4. How I tested it (~250 words)
- Explain the setup like you would to a smart coworker over coffee.
- What did your first plan look like, and what was wrong with it?
- What did you change, and why?
- What surprised you while building it?

## 5. What happened (~250 words)
- One table or chart. Which one, and why that one?
- What does it show, in plain words?
- Was anything different from what you expected? Say so directly.
- Anything you noticed that you didn't plan to test?

## 6. What I'd do with this (~125 words)
- If you were building an agent tomorrow, what would you do differently?
- Who should care most, and who can ignore this?

## 7. What I can't claim yet (~100 words)
- What didn't you test that someone might assume you did?
- What would you test next?
- Link to the full brief.

---

## Before posting: quick read-aloud check
- Does it sound like you'd say it out loud to a coworker?
- Cut any sentence that only exists to sound smart or to transition.
- Every number traces back to the brief.
